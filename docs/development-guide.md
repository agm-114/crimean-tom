# Development guide

This is the handoff document for an agent or contributor entering the project without prior thread context. Read `AGENTS.md` first because its scope and testing rules are mandatory.

## Product boundary

Crimean Tom owns the operations layer between Sea Power battles. The intended loop is:

1. Load the persistent commander and task force.
2. Offer a small set of generated contracts.
3. Generate one complete Sea Power mission from the selected force and contract.
4. Let Sea Power run the battle without live campaign synchronization.
5. Read the native debrief and persistent task-force snapshot.
6. Apply losses, expenditure, salvage, repair, rearm, purchases, group changes, and fatigue.
7. Advance the campaign turn and offer new contracts.

Do not add a campaign server, background war simulation, live unit streaming, a companion process, or a server-authoritative tactical simulation unless the project direction changes explicitly.

The campaign state class is split into `CampaignOperationsState.cs` for lifecycle, `CampaignEconomy.cs` for purchases and salvage, `CampaignReadiness.cs` for groups and refit, `CampaignPersistence.cs` for save keys and serialization, and `CampaignDebug.cs` for autoresolve. These are partial declarations of the same type. `Campaign/NativeCampaignAccess.cs` wraps the private native snapshot, purchase, reward, save, and mission-presentation APIs. It caches reflected campaign members but reads the current singleton on each call. Keep reflection out of campaign rules; Harmony patch-target lookups stay in the patches.

`Scenarios/ScenarioMissionWriter.cs` contains the fleet-clash writer and shared mission headers, unit counts, startup triggers, and opposed objective declarations. Route missions pass their extra protected ships and formations to the common header writer. `Scenarios/MissionTrigger.cs` owns arrival/loss condition and outcome serialization through `WriteTo()`. Route, passage, and fleet-clash generators supply their thresholds, messages, and objectives. An area condition counts arrivals; a condition without an area counts destroyed units. Keep `Trigger2`, `Trigger3`, and `Condition1` names stable because campaign remapping reads them.

`Scenarios/ScenarioRouteCorridor.cs` shares route/passage corridor geometry, deployments, and group retasking. Callers choose the attack lane: route operations consume one random draw, while passage uses the lateral offset's sign without drawing. Protected-group creation and mission objectives stay in their respective generators.

`Scenarios/MissionWriter.cs` writes INI sections and typed values in memory, with invariant numeric formatting, comma-separated lists, position formatting, and escaped newlines. It is not a parser and does not touch Sea Power's global INI cache. Keep native `IniHandler` for reading and editing campaign files. `Fleet/FleetDeployment.cs` owns deployed ship and aircraft serialization through `WriteTo(MissionWriter)`; formations provide `ToMissionValue()`. Protected convoy records also write their own fields. Generators choose the units and orders, then ask these records to write them.

`Scenarios/MissionWaypoint.cs` owns typed positions, waypoints, weapon-status orders, and attack-at-waypoint orders. Routes stay as `IReadOnlyList<MissionWaypoint>` until written. Position coordinates are X, altitude, Z; optional named depth overrides altitude for submarines. Formatting preserves horizontal `0.##`, altitude `0`, pipe-separated routes, and colon-separated attack targets. A null route omits the key so formation followers keep their native behavior.

`Scenarios/CivilianTrafficUnit.cs` shares civilian ship and aircraft serialization, including their crossing waypoint. `AppendCivilianTraffic` keeps the separate spawn rules and seeded random draw order; the record selects the native variant/squadron key and telegraph setting.

`Scenarios/MissionUnitReferences.cs` owns hull numbering and the reference matchers used by campaign remapping. `NumberHulls()` numbers vessels and submarines separately in source order, before tactical grouping. Deployment, destruction-value objectives, and persistent instance IDs use that same sequence. The campaign ID map uses the classified player force, so a submarine identified only by its native folder still receives a submarine reference. `FormatHull()` also handles protected convoy offsets. Matching preserves existing word boundaries, section line endings, and the different hull/aircraft case rules.

Inside `FleetOrderOfBattlePlanner`, one `EmbarkedAirDeployment` builder per fleet side owns aircraft/helicopter counters and output collections. `EmbarkedAirStation` carries the host ship and geometry. Carrier CAP, AEW, and ASW are added in that order. If the carrier cannot provide ASW helicopters, or the group has no carrier, one helicopter-equipped escort supplies the patrol. Counters span all groups on the side. CAP and AEW leaders loop their routes; sonobuoy-laying routes do not loop.

Use `aircraft.WriteTo(mission)` or `deployment.WriteTo(mission)` to write unit sections. Use `mission.Write("Heading", heading, "0")` for a formatted number, `mission.WritePosition("RelativePositionInNM", new(x, altitude, z))` for a position, and `mission.WriteWaypoints(route)` for orders. Pass interpolated values directly to `Write` so its `FormattableString` overload formats numbers with invariant culture.

`UI/` contains the native overlays, shared `NativeUi` styling and campaign-window closing, and `ChoicePicker<T>`. The picker binds string labels to Noesis while keeping values typed in C#, so setup options do not depend on parallel selected-index switches. The scenario families share one setup form but retain their existing options and defaults.

Folders do not change the `CrimeanTom.SeaPower` namespace, reflected type names or deployment locations. The project already enables SDK implicit usings; avoid repeating those imports in every file.

The three longer documents under `docs/` are design references. They describe intended behavior and should not be treated as proof that every feature is implemented.

## Build and deployment

The current generation work is tracked in [the scenario and campaign plan](scenario-and-campaign-generation-plan.md). Code implementation is complete; its live acceptance checklist is still open. `ShippingRoutes.cs` contains eight bidirectional port routes and local encounter geometry, including coastal transit and selected chokepoints. `ScenarioTerrain.cs` reads installed base DEM tiles for conservative placement checks. Those checks do not include terrain stamps or prove formation navigation in battle. The North Sea route is too shallow for the current 80-metre submarine clearance; unsupported layouts fail with an explanation.

`ScenarioStartingOptions.cs` owns shared aircraft/order settings, protected-ship control and difficulty definitions. `ScenarioStartingControls.cs` presents these in standalone setup and `CampaignPreparationWindow.cs`. Protected ships default to AI control; the toggle applies only to escort play, never observers or interdiction. Player-controlled protected ships obey the preload-orders setting. `CampaignScenarioSettings.cs` persists the toggles and reads the native campaign difficulty selection. Campaign difficulty changes offer generation, not purchase prices. Explicit infeasible composition/budget combinations fail with a readable error; random composition may fall back to surface-focused forces.

`CampaignAircraftStock.cs` trims authored patrols against the final native air roster. Its narrowly scoped reconciliation patch reuses the game's stock/ammunition handling for the first generated contract. Verify that patch and aircraft ownership across a live first sortie and save/load before changing aircraft preparation again.

`RouteInterceptionPlanner.cs` assigns surface pursuit/interception lanes and submarine ambush stations before mission writing. It validates actual route bends and formation-offset paths against the base DEM. Route convoys use telegraph 3 and line astern; attacking surface groups use telegraph 4. `ScenarioUnitCatalog.SpeedAtTelegraph()` mirrors the native authored-speed/default-ladder selection. `RouteChaseBudget.cs` caps pursuit credit at 20%, considers installed weapon reach and closing delay, and accepts a whole-hull change only if the revised placement still supports it. Campaign interdiction reduces the generated escort budget instead of changing owned attackers.

Pursuit requires every group member to be at most 60 metres long and 500 tons, with known surface-weapon reach no greater than 12 NM and an operating-speed advantage of at least eight knots. Unsafe or impractical chases fall back to flank stations. Larger ships never receive pursuit stations. Surface placements clear known weapon reach by at least eight NM, or five NM for pursuit boats, against the initial convoy and escort positions. Unknown reach uses an 80 NM buffer and cannot qualify for pursuit. Separate attacker formations keep at least two NM between hulls; members within a formation remain 0.75 NM apart. Torpedo range parsing includes authored `SpeedSettingRanges`.

`ScenarioActivityPlan.cs` builds independent patrols and coastal sites within a combined per-side allowance. Standalone setup exposes presence and size controls. Campaign offers use fixed Random activity generated from the board's saved ready-roster budget; preparation cannot reroll it. The offer displays any minimum deployment required by its activity. Nearby units receive no campaign ownership tags and do not count toward main objectives. `CampaignMissionData` preserves friendly patrol submarines across native preparation, which otherwise replaces all player submarine sections. `MissionWriter` keeps sections in memory so optional activity can update unit counts without duplicate sections or keys.

Standalone setup accepts a replay seed. Use the same settings and installed data when reproducing it. Read the generated mission's `[CrimeanTom]` section for budgets and per-group interception diagnostics.

Random-enabled dropdowns label the random entry simply `Random` and show roll chances beside concrete choices. `ScenarioRandomization.cs` shares sea-state, composition, presence and nearby-size weights with the UI. Nearby size now rolls the listed 10%/25%/40% allowances at 60%/30%/10%, replacing the continuous 5–40% draw. Region odds count viable theater entries, including both North Atlantic entries; encounter odds update for the selected region's routes and chokepoints. Composition odds describe the initial roll, before unavailable or infeasible submarine profiles fall back to surface forces. Standalone friendly naval activity and both coastal-site controls default to On, with placement still required; enemy naval activity defaults to Off. Campaign activity remains fixed by its existing Random policy at offer generation.

`Generated Route Operation Observer` watches an escort battle with Blue alignment and no player-controlled hulls. Observer truth is sent only to the player's plotting table. Delegated friendly units receive refreshed native tracks, including aircraft launched after the initial transfer. Unload clears the tracked allied task force and restores the previous truth-display setting.

Route charts show three separated 12-NM search areas, a convoy-start marker, the local exit, and the complete shipping route with named origin and destination markers at its offshore approaches. `RouteChart6` uses the native `ThreatArrow` symbol's open `MapPolyline`, including every authored route bend and a direction marker at the destination. It has no polygon or filled corridor. Native `MissionBriefingMapInitialLatitude`, `MissionBriefingMapInitialLongitude` and `MissionBriefingMapInitialZoom` fit the full crossing with label margins. Without these fields the game defaults to a 750-NM half-span. Long crossings therefore start zoomed out; zoom in to inspect the local encounter. Symbol spacing and preview bounds account for map projection, independently of the game's tactical placement grid.

## Runtime flow

At startup, `Plugin.TriggerEntryPoint` writes the roster, installs all Harmony patches, writes the direct-launch debug scenarios, and logs the mod version and assembly path.

The campaign uses four always-visible native Task Force Mode entries: fleet clash, route escort, route interdiction, and strait passage. Selecting an entry shows the turn's offer in its native briefing, including region, enemy coalition, broad capability estimates, protected-group variant, payment and salvage terms. `CampaignContractOffers` generates opponents from a saved campaign seed, turn and mission index. The four entries include small, medium and large jobs. Their enemies do not scale with the player's deployment. Reopening an entry or reloading the same campaign does not reroll its terms. Advancing the turn refreshes offers. This is still the native four-entry screen, not a separate contract-board UI.

Immediately before Sea Power's `LinearCampaignTaskForceMissionGenerator.TryGenerate` runs, `CampaignScenarioGenerator` replaces the selected installed template using that offer and the actual launch snapshot. The player's force replaces one side of the predetermined matchup. Campaign seas default to state 1, traffic is off and there is no deadline. Standalone skirmish balancing and setup options are unchanged.

Sea Power's native generator then adds the persistent player ships, aircraft, damage, ammunition, crew state, and `CampaignTag` instance identifiers. `CampaignMissionData` matches those identifiers to the saved template plan, remaps objective references, and restores tactical groups and orders within each campaign group. Convoy and air formations are retained. Passage missions receive waypoints into the exit area after this mapping. Generation or validation errors block launch through the native warning dialog.

After battle, `LinearCampaignTaskForceDebriefViewModel.SaveDebriefSnapshot` is the handoff point. `CampaignOperationsState.RecordBattle` reads the native hostile-kill records, awards salvage shares, creates market offers, advances fatigue, and marks management as pending. Closing the debrief opens `CampaignManagementWindow`. Attempting another mission while management is pending opens the same window and blocks the launch.

Finishing management clears Sea Power's per-mission one-time application markers for the completed reusable mission index, resets that mission's completion state, advances the campaign turn, and saves the campaign.

Pressing F8 on the Crimean Tom campaign map opens `CampaignAutoresolveWindow`. It can write a deterministic native-shaped victory or defeat debrief for any of the four contracts and then enters the same `RecordBattle` and management path used after a tactical battle. This is a campaign-layer diagnostic tool: it deliberately does not simulate tactical damage, ammunition expenditure, combat duration, or friendly losses.

## Persistent state

Crimean Tom stores its state in Sea Power's existing `LinearCampaign.PersistentData` dictionary. Current keys are:

- `CrimeanTomTurn`
- `CrimeanTomSalvageShares`
- `CrimeanTomActiveMission`
- `CrimeanTomPendingBattle`
- `CrimeanTomRecordedBattle`
- `CrimeanTomMarketOffers`
- `CrimeanTomClaimedSalvage`
- `CrimeanTomGroupAssignments`
- `CrimeanTomFatigue`
- `CrimeanTomAutoresolveCounter`
- `CrimeanTomContractSeed`
- `CrimeanTomFirstSortie`
- `CrimeanTomAircraftDeployed`, `CrimeanTomPreloadOrders`
- `CrimeanTomActivityBoard`, `CrimeanTomActivityBudget`

Difficulty uses the native `TaskForceDifficultyPreset` key. The activity board stores its budget before preparation so reopening a campaign does not change nearby strength or contract payment. The offer seed is retried only while finding a valid initial layout, then held for that turn.

Unit assignments and readiness delays are keyed by Sea Power's persistent instance ID, not class name. Moving between Group 1, Group 2 and Reserve is free and does not change readiness. Recovered units enter Reserve with two refit turns; purchases enter Reserve with one workup turn. They can be assigned to an active group immediately but cannot deploy until ready. Combat does not add operational fatigue yet. The existing `CrimeanTomFatigue` key now represents acquisition/refit delays only.

Reserve and refitting ships never deploy. If all owned hulls are unavailable, launching opens port management so the player can assign ready reserves or wait one turn for workups. Waiting grants no points, salvage or fresh market stock. It advances the turn and contract offers, and is disabled when there are no outstanding workups.

Hull purchasing through the native builder closes after the first sortie. Native aircraft purchasing, repair, rearm and loadout preparation remain available. The post-battle market offers two distinct ship classes at list price with the saved commander discount for eligible national variants. Unclaimed offers expire on finishing management.

Contract payment is `size base + ceil(enemy list value * 0.15) + family premium`. Size bases are 25/40/60, premiums are 0 for clash, 5 for route operations and 10 for passage. Victory also increases point cap by 10/20/30 and grants salvage shares equal to half payment, rounded up. Draws and defeats grant no contract payment, cap increase or salvage shares. Native CSAR rewards and civilian penalties remain separate. Victory can award salvage shares even without kills. At most one destroyed hostile hull may be recovered per battle, at half its list value in shares, rounded up. Surviving enemy register rows are not eligible. Unspent shares carry forward; unclaimed hulls expire.

`RequiredResult=CompleteDefeat` is intentional: the native game otherwise refuses to write failed-battle debriefs. `CampaignPatches` gates both debrief reward reads and the shared native reward application method, including the earlier post-state writer. It disables the campaign-map fallback payment. Do not replace this with a higher RequiredResult and lose defeat persistence.

Campaign fleet clashes use per-hull one-shot destruction triggers to add undiscounted ship value to native variables. A side breaks at 75% lost value. `CampaignValueObjectives` writes these triggers before native renumbering. Passage designates the most valuable hulls covering at least 60% of fleet value; every designated hull must reach the exit, and losing any one fails the passage. Escorts still receive exit routes. Convoy objectives remain cargo-count based.

## Native API inspection

Sea Power campaign types are not all public. The integration therefore uses Harmony and `AccessTools` against the installed assembly:

```text
Sea Power_Data\Managed\Seapower-Scripts.dll
```

Important types:

- `SeapowerUI.ViewModels.LinearCampaignTaskForceSaveManager`
- `SeapowerUI.ViewModels.LinearCampaignTaskForceDebriefViewModel`
- `SeapowerUI.ViewModels.LinearCampaignTaskForceMissionGenerator`
- `SeapowerUI.ViewModels.LinearCampaignMapViewModel`
- `SeapowerUI.ViewModels.LinearCampaignTaskForceBuilderViewModel`
- `SeaPower.LinearCampaign`
- `SeaPower.LinearCampaignMission`

Use `ilspycmd` to verify signatures against the installed game before changing a reflected or patched member. Examples:

```powershell
ilspycmd -t SeapowerUI.ViewModels.LinearCampaignTaskForceSaveManager 'C:\Program Files (x86)\Steam\steamapps\common\Sea Power\Sea Power_Data\Managed\Seapower-Scripts.dll'
ilspycmd -t SeaPower.LinearCampaignMission 'C:\Program Files (x86)\Steam\steamapps\common\Sea Power\Sea Power_Data\Managed\Seapower-Scripts.dll'
```

Native state lookup assumes increasing mission indexes. We store the latest result for each surviving ship under `TaskForceMode_CrimeanTom_UnitState_<instanceId>`, including its damage child sections. The `TaskForceMode_` prefix lets the native save/load code preserve these records. The latest-state hook directs both native repair and launch preparation to them, so playing contract 4 then contract 1 carries forward the same ship state.

Only after management completes, clear both `Mission_<index>_*` and `TaskForceMode_Mission<index>_*`, including old launch/post snapshots and completion-reward flags. Remove the index from the three native record sets as well. Do not delete the current roster or the latest per-ship state. Real debrief salvage sections use `Mission_<index>_TaskForceDebrief_Kill_<row>`. The debug writer must use that exact schema.

## Known risks and next work

- The 2026-09-07 gameplay pass passed a build and 326 in-memory mission checks using installed aircraft/loadout data and fixture fleets. Loose-follow screens, looping air patrols, carrier-free ASW cover, and revised objective text still need live verification. No game was launched because desktop control was unavailable.
- Convoy screen orders follow the initial protected leader. Verify native formation succession after that ship sinks; the escort briefing tells the player to retask the screen. In interdiction play, the AI defender must be checked too.
- Generated route operations use base-DEM checks, but terrain stamps and native formation movement still need live inspection. Other scenario families do not share the full route-placement validation.
- Minesweeper escort protects a moving group; it does not create a minefield or simulate clearance. Civilian traffic adds contacts, not an attributed civilian-casualty scoring rule.
- The management loop has passed a native-UI, two-turn autoresolve test. A two-battle test using real tactical debriefs and a save/load boundary is still required.
- The latest-state and mission-reference fixes still need full native battle/debrief/save/load verification.
- The campaign shows four native contract nodes with fixed-per-turn briefings rather than a dedicated contract-board UI. Confirm offer text, payment and actual opponents agree in a real launch.
- Fixed threat bands and payment rates need playtesting against actual fleet costs. Buying stronger ships is intended to make existing jobs easier.
- The campaign map preview comes from minimal source templates and may not reflect the final randomized battle until launch.
- Fatigue filtering occurs in the launch snapshot, so earlier native UI may still preview the full owned force.
- Market offers are ships only. Submarines can appear in salvage if the debrief records them.
- The latest design adjustments still need full UI and real victory/defeat payment checks.
- Carrier aircraft, route-operation behavior, friendly AI detachments, and group orders need native gameplay verification after any generator change.
- Reflected and patched members are version-sensitive. Recheck them whenever Sea Power updates.

Keep fixes bounded. Prove one complete real-battle campaign cycle before adding more contract families or deeper management systems.
