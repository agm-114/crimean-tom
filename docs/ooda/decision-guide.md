# OODA problem solving and prompt techniques

The runtime gets the current player briefing, revealed updates, and player-visible state. Use this guide to develop prompts and tests across missions. The examples are situations the AI should recognize from its inputs, including in a scenario it has never seen.

| Situation | What to look at | How to respond |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Two required ships have a shared arrival deadline. | Each ship's route, speed, estimated arrival, and the time basis in the briefing. | Plan around the later arrival. Keep a valid route in place. Seek a new route or replan if the expected arrival slips or the leg is blocked. |
| A surface track closes on an escorted merchant and ignores a hail. | Its latest bearing and motion, classification, current rules of engagement, and screen coverage. | Keep it unknown. Monitor or investigate if the geometry makes it relevant and the escort can stay covered. An unanswered hail alone gives no authorization to fire. |
| Passive sonar produces a new ambiguous contact. | The detecting unit, bearing, freshness, possible classes, and whether range or position is actually known. | Preserve ambiguity. Seek a better observation from a suitable unit or a change of observation geometry if available. Require a valid target and weapon solution before committing an attack. |
| A previously tracked submarine drops off the picture. | Last detected area, observed movement, age, and uncertainty. | Search around the last known area if the mission warrants it. Treat extrapolation as a search lead and reassess when a new observation arrives. |
| Passive and shared sensors leave an important approach uncovered. | Which observer can fill the gap, what emitting would reveal, and effective standing orders. | Use a permitted active sensor when the information would change the next decision. Give the search a purpose and revisit it once that purpose is met. |
| A strike and an air patrol need the same flight deck. | Aircraft already airborne, preparation and launch estimates, travel time, remaining fuel, and the recovery window. | Keep a plan for relief and identify any gap before committing the strike. Use live Deck Tweaks readiness and account for the delay of a loadout swap. |
| A damaged ship may lose its remaining missiles. | Own damage, native defenses, threat evidence, authorized targets, and friendly salvos already inbound. | Ask Luna only if there is a real choice and time to act. Consider a limited shot when it advances the objective and may be the last useful opportunity. Keep native defenses running. |
| A new message changes the mission or a native order fails. | The newest player-visible instruction, current assignments, and the observed reason for failure. | Keep tasks that still help. Replan the affected groups and log the observation that prompted the change. |

Keep identity, location quality, and authority separate. A briefing report or possible class guides a search. A good position estimate still may lack identification. A manual change in displayed posture supplies a different fact from a native identification. The game combines observations into a plotted picture with missing fields and uncertainty. Use those fields as shown and read live weapon and sensor constraints for the current build. [Triassic Games on sensors and plotting](https://www.triassicgames.com/2022/12/30/dev-diary-7-sensors-and-plotting-in-sea-power/) · [PMC tactical display](https://sea-power.pmctactical.org/tactical-display.php) · [Installed game mechanics](game-mechanics.md)

Use the general guide as trusted instructions and put the briefing and observations in a separate data payload. Give each role the objective, effective limits, current orders, and the IDs of actions it can take. Ask for one short reason tied to evidence in the input. When a missing observation could change the answer, let Sol make a scoped lookup. Give Luna the observations and legal options up front when the deadline is tight.

Test by changing one fact at a time. Rename the tracks and reorder the list to check that a decision follows the evidence. Replace a firm fix with a bearing or remove identification to check whether the model seeks more information. Remove authorization after a reply to check that the native adapter blocks the shot. Give the same situation a different briefing objective to test whether it changes priorities for the right reason. Use names and IDs created for each run.

Use guide tactics as heuristics. Live sensors and conditions determine the useful search geometry, especially in shallow water. A report can represent an aircraft, surface ship, submarine, or sensor event. Ship type alone is weak evidence. The game's standing orders, crew and fuel status, and visible coverage are more useful to the prompt than hardcoded range tables.
