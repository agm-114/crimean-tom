Use these general Sea Power tactics when the supplied observations and available actions support them.

- **Transit and escort.** Plan around the slower required ship, navigable water, the deadline, and the route the brief calls for. Watch the units being protected. Move a screen to meet a credible threat only when you can keep enough coverage on the protected ships or route.
- **Screen high-value units.** Use the briefing and current plan to identify units the mission depends on. When an exposed approach warrants a screen, consider sending a less mission-critical unit ahead or toward the likely threat axis if it can better withstand or avoid the expected attack. Choose a screen with the sensors to find emerging threats and the weapons to engage them or cue a shooter in time. Keep it close enough to help, and give the protected unit room to turn away. Reassess the route if the screen cannot handle what it finds.
- **Detection and identification.** Track how recently a contact was seen and what kind of observation it is. A bearing or a last known area can guide a search. A class estimate, emitter, or unanswered challenge does not establish allegiance. If identification matters, seek another observation or use an available identification request.
- **Sensors and standing orders.** Weigh a better picture against the cost of emitting. Favor passive or shared coverage when it is adequate. Use active search for a clear purpose when permitted. Respect the game's effective formation and unit orders and leave routine defenses to the game.
- **Engagement.** Use the game's available actions against current threats. Consider readiness, firing limits, tracking, and fire already committed. Update a stale track before taking a shot that needs a current solution.
- **Undersea search.** Search ahead of a protected route or around the last known contact area. Use the sensor coverage, communications, and endurance shown in the current game. Keep enough aircraft fuel to recover.
- **Flight operations.** With Deck Tweaks, preparation, ready, and airborne are different states. Include preparation, launch and transit time when planning relief for a patrol. Account for crew, stores, loadout changes, and recovery fuel. Avoid changing a useful loadout without a reason tied to the mission.
