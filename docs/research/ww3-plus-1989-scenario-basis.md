# WW3++ 1989 scenario research and provenance

## Purpose and method

This file records the external evidence behind **Fulda Gap Gone Hot — Global
War**, the deliberately oversized Crimean Tom validation campaign. It is not an
exact 11 September 1989 order of battle. It is an alternate-history general-war
mobilization constrained by late-Cold-War force totals, known bases and
infrastructure, contemporary alliance relationships, and 1989 commercial
shipping scale.

Every translation below is labelled as one of:

- **historical anchor** — directly supported by a linked government or
  intergovernmental source;
- **representative abstraction** — one campaign entity stands for a ship,
  formation, mission package, installation component, or traffic sample;
- **authored extrapolation** — a plausible WW3++ wartime disposition that is
  not asserted as the real location of a named unit on a particular day.

Sources were accessed on 23 August 2026. Wherever possible, the register uses
the publishing institution's own archive rather than a secondary summary.

## Scale anchors

### Naval forces

The US Naval History and Heritage Command records **592 active US Navy ships on
30 September 1989**: 14 carriers, 40 cruisers, 68 destroyers, 100 frigates, 99
attack submarines, 36 SSBNs, 61 amphibious ships, 137 auxiliaries, and smaller
categories. The same official history describes the late-Cold-War force as 15
carrier battle groups, four battleship surface action groups, and roughly 100
attack submarines by 1990. Those totals make the old 60-entity whole-world seed
unsuitable even as a representative engineering load. Sources:
[US ship force levels](https://www.history.navy.mil/research/histories/ship-histories/us-ship-force-levels.html)
and [The Navy in the Cold War Era, 1945–1991](https://www.history.navy.mil/research/library/online-reading-room/title-list-alphabetically/n/the-navy-in-the-cold-war-era-1945-1991.html).

The scenario does not reproduce all 592 US hulls. It places representative
carrier, cruiser, destroyer, frigate, submarine, amphibious, and auxiliary
packages on both US coasts and at Hawaii. This is a **representative
abstraction** and **authored wartime dispersal**, sized to expose scheduler,
projection, persistence, streaming, and Operational-simulation costs.

The US East Coast concentration is anchored by Norfolk's historical role as
home of the Atlantic Fleet and its Cold-War piers, aviation, storage, and fuel
infrastructure. Mayport became a naval air station in 1988 and supported major
surface and air warfare activity. Sources:
[Naval Station Norfolk](https://www.history.navy.mil/browse-by-topic/organization-and-administration/installations/naval-station-norfolk.html)
and [Naval Station Mayport](https://www.history.navy.mil/content/history/nhhc/browse-by-topic/organization-and-administration/installations/naval-station-mayport.html).

The West Coast and Pacific concentration is anchored by San Diego, Long Beach,
Alameda, Puget Sound/Bremerton, Bangor, Whidbey Island, and Pearl Harbor. It is
not a claim that every generated hull was in port. Official ship histories
place major exercises and maintenance activity across San Diego, Long Beach,
Alameda, Bremerton, and the North Pacific in 1989; PACEX 89 assembled three
carrier and two battleship groups. Sources:
[Naval Base San Diego](https://www.history.navy.mil/content/history/nhhc/browse-by-topic/organization-and-administration/installations/naval-base-san-diego.html),
[Enterprise 1986–1990](https://www.history.navy.mil/research/histories/ship-histories/danfs/e/enterprise-cvan-65-viii-1986-1990.html),
and [Antietam](https://www.history.navy.mil/research/histories/ship-histories/danfs/a/antietam-iii.html).

### Central Front and reinforcement

NATO's contemporary public force comparison describes a Warsaw Pact advantage
in forward-deployed conventional forces and a NATO dependence on
transatlantic reinforcement. A US Army historical study records that the 1989
M+10 concept depended on European POMCUS stocks, while a 469,000-short-ton
Central Region equipment shortfall and limited airlift made additional sealift
necessary. These are **historical anchors** for the dense Central Front,
POMCUS/logistics sites, reinforcement airfields, and Atlantic convoy traffic.
Sources:
[NATO–Warsaw Pact force comparison, 1987](https://archives.nato.int/force-comparison-1987-nato-and-warsaw-pact),
[Conventional forces in Europe — the facts](https://archives.nato.int/uploads/r/null/1/4/141514/CONVENTIONAL_FORCES_IN_EUROPE_-_THE_FACTS_ENG.pdf),
and [The Army Command Post and Defense Reshaping](https://history.army.mil/portals/143/Images/Publications/catalog/40-4-1.pdf).

The campaign represents individual air-defense, airfield, radar, depot, and
shore-defense components, but corps/division warfare remains abstract. The
exact number and placement of generated formations are **representative
abstractions**, not a digitized NATO or Warsaw Pact classified OOB.

### Cuba

Cuba is explicitly Soviet-aligned in the scenario. A declassified CIA study
described Cuba as the USSR's most valuable overseas intelligence base,
identified the Lourdes SIGINT complex, and recorded Soviet support that
upgraded Cuban SA-2/SA-3 defenses, added SA-6 and SA-9 systems, improved command
and control, and supplied MiG-23 fighters and ASW helicopters. A second CIA
assessment warned that Cuban forces could threaten merchant shipping through
the Caribbean and Gulf exits and divert US forces from Atlantic reinforcement.
These are **historical anchors** for the Cuba region, Lourdes, Havana/Mariel,
Cienfuegos, San Antonio de los Baños, Holguín, layered GBAD, coastal defense,
air formations, naval patrols, and pressure on the Florida Straits. Sources:
[The Cuban-Soviet Connection](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/CIA-RDP04T00794R000100080001-0.pdf)
and [Cuba: Soviet Military](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/CIA-RDP85S00317R000100110005-3.pdf).

The scenario's exact opening missions, site coordinates, three-layer defense
complexes, and movement orders are **authored extrapolations**. They are meant
to create an engineering and gameplay stressor, not reveal or reconstruct
classified site plans.

### Civilian traffic and sealift

UNCTAD's 1989 review records a world merchant fleet carrying 1.39 billion tons
of oil, 630 million tons of dry bulk, and 1.63 billion tons in its residual
fleet measure, with very large tanker, bulk, and general-cargo ton-mile totals.
The former handful of convoy counters could not validate contact load,
deconfliction, protection, or unintended-effects behavior at that scale.
Source: [Review of Maritime Transport 1989](https://unctad.org/system/files/official-document/rmt1989_en.pdf).

The campaign samples 90 civilian, merchant, fishing, sealift, and auxiliary
entities across Atlantic and Pacific routes. This is a **representative traffic
sample**, not a literal global AIS picture. Until the campaign has diplomacy
and noncombatant rules, traffic is assigned to Western or Soviet-aligned
protection scopes. The absence of genuinely neutral traffic is an explicit
model gap, not a historical claim.

## Airbase, radar-net, GBAD, and A2/AD translation

Air Force counterair doctrine defines an integrated air-defense system as
coordinated active and passive defenses ranging from guns and MANPADS through
complex, redundant radar and missile systems. Air University research also
connects airbase security, engineering, fuel, maintenance, and logistics to
sortie generation and recovery. These modern doctrinal sources are used for
**system decomposition**, not to claim that a 2020s doctrine document describes
the exact 1989 network. Sources:
[AFDP 3-01 counterair planning](https://www.doctrine.af.mil/Portals/61/documents/Annex_3-01/3-01-D09-AIR-Planning.PDF)
and [Defending Air Bases in an Age of Insurgency, volume II](https://www.airuniversity.af.edu/Portals/10/AUPress/Books/B_0154_CAUDILL_DEFENDING_AIR_BASES_IN_AN_AGE_OF_INSURGENCY_VOL_II.PDF).

Each of 20 opening airbase defense complexes therefore contains:

1. a streamable airfield/runway and operations object;
2. an early-warning radar;
3. short-, medium-, and long-range GBAD layers;
4. protected fuel and ammunition sites;
5. home-based opening fighter, strike, and support sortie packages.

The larger theater also contains additional radar/SAM packages, shore-defense
batteries, ports, depots, fleet auxiliaries, and convoy routes. Fixed-site and
ground catalog objects are tactically eligible so future ground streaming is
tested by the data model. Each of the 26 army/corps/division-scale abstractions
also projects two physical armor targets and one low-end SHORAD target into its
task group. The parent remains the Operational maneuver/sustainment model and
cannot attack its own target projection; the physical objects exist primarily
for aircraft, helicopters, bombers, missiles, and naval gunfire to attack. Only
the 26 parent abstractions retain an Operational maximum-fidelity ceiling.

Strategic SAMs, early-warning radars, and ballistic-missile launchers are
resident systems: the fidelity scheduler keeps their coupled islands Tactical
whenever a compatible live worker has capacity, even outside player interest.
If no worker can materialize them, they remain Operational rather than falling
to Strategic, so the campaign analogue continues to detect and engage targets.
Sea Power's installed catalog has no period ballistic TEL. The opening four
NATO and six Warsaw Pact TELs therefore use clearly named, catalog-backed
SAM-launcher silhouettes as temporary visual proxies while retaining explicit
500 km and 650 km ballistic capabilities in the campaign model.

The current engine does **not** yet implement a full air-tasking cycle. Opening
air entities and CAP/patrol orders validate ownership, movement, contact,
streaming, and load. A finished airbase system still needs aircraft inventory,
mission planning, takeoff/recovery, turnaround time, fuel and magazine draw,
runway damage and repair, dispersal, suppression/destruction of enemy air
defenses, and sortie regeneration.

## Installed catalog provenance

The seed was checked against the installed Sea Power
`0.8.2-23444+s358` English catalog on 23 August 2026. It uses real identifiers
from `vessel_names.ini`, `aircraft_names.ini`, and `land_units_names.ini`,
including US and Soviet ship classes; civilian freighters, Ro/Ro ships,
trawlers, and fishing vessels; NATO and Warsaw Pact aircraft; airfields; NATO
MIM-23/Nike/Skyguard components; Soviet SA-2/3/5/6/10/11 components; radars;
coastal missiles; fuel tanks; ammunition depots; and warehouses.

Catalog presence proves that Sea Power has an object definition, not that the
runtime bridge can already materialize and synchronize every family. The
scenario intentionally leaves those entities tactical-eligible so missing
streaming support is visible as work rather than silently excluded.
